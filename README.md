# jobshield
JobShield: one toolkit for job seekers, from “is this job real?” to “I got the offer”
SaaS idea plan for Salonik Lotubulua, November 20, 2026

A global, multi-service product with no WhatsApp and no local-market dependence. It sells in USD to people in countries where prices can be higher than in Kenya.

Why this problem is genuine
Job seekers lose real money to fake offers, and the numbers are growing. In the US, the FTC reports that job scams grew to over 105,000 reports in 2024, nearly three times the 2020 level, with reported losses above $513 million. The Better Business Bureau says employment scams more than doubled in 2025, helped by the popularity of remote work and AI-made fake listings and recruiter messages. One summary of the FBI’s 2025 report counts 24,688 employment-scam complaints and $362.9 million in reported losses.

These are US-reported figures, and many victims never report, so the real total is higher and the global picture is unknown. Reported losses do not show how many people would pay for a tool, so the demand is still unproven.

The services (one login)
Is this job real? The user pastes a recruiter message, email, or job post, and gets a risk verdict with reasons: fees up front, a cheque that must be cashed, interviews only on chat apps, crypto, an email domain that doesn’t match the company.
Offer letter review. The user pastes an offer or contract, and it flags unpaid trials, equipment fees, odd non-competes, and vague pay terms in plain language.
CV tailoring. It adjusts a CV and cover letter to a specific job post, clearly and honestly, without inventing experience.
Application tracker. It records each application and reminds the user when to follow up.
Interview prep. It generates likely questions for the specific job and gives feedback on practice answers.
Services 1 and 2 are the hook, because people feel the fear before they feel the need for CV help. Services 3 to 5 keep them using the product while they search.

Why a bundle makes sense here
Natural flow. A job seeker moves through all five steps, so one product covers the whole path.
Shared data. The CV, the job posts, and the application list feed every service.
Pricing: a free plan (3 checks a month), and a paid plan around $7 to $12 a month or a one-time 30-day “job hunt pass” for about $19. A pass fits better because people stop needing it once they’re hired, so subscriptions would churn naturally.
Distribution: pages like “is this recruiter message a scam?” can attract search traffic, and the free checker is easy to share. The Remote Key job board could be a first place to show it.
Honest limits
Crowded in parts. Tools like Teal, Huntr, and Jobscan already handle tracking and CV matching, and general scam checkers exist. Their current features are unchecked. The safety-first angle plus one login for the whole path is the only wedge, and it is unproven.
Wrong verdicts have consequences. Telling someone a real job is a scam, or a scam is safe, can cost them. Wording must say “red flags found” or “no red flags found,” never “safe,” and always link to official checks.
Scammers adapt, so the rules need regular updating.
People’s CVs are personal data, so privacy and clear notices matter, especially with a free external AI model.
Job seekers churn fast, and the price points are guesses.

Test it in two days
Post in a few job-seeker communities where self-promotion is allowed: “Paste a suspicious job message and I’ll tell you if it looks like a scam, free.” Do 20 by hand with a free AI chat. Count how many then ask about their CV or offer, and ask 5 whether they’d pay $9 for a 30-day pass. If few reply, drop it.
